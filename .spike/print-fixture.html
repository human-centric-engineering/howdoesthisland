§ 04

Recommendations

  1. 4 · Pathos · Stakes: pain and desire · high impact

    Stakes: pain and desire scores 1 of 4. Describe the specific moment the reader realises the current way is not working, in their words.

    Move: Name the struggling moment

    For example

    Accounting software for small businesses.

    Rewrite

    It is 11pm on the last day of the quarter and you are still matching receipts to bank lines.

  2. 1 · Ethos · Identification and shared identity · medium impact

    Identification and shared identity scores 1 of 4. Briefly share an experience that puts the author in the reader's shoes.

    Move: Show you have been there

    For example

    We help founders raise capital.

    Rewrite

    I pitched 41 investors before my first yes. Here is what I wish I had known.

  3. 5 · Pathos · Identity, status and belonging · medium impact

    Identity, status and belonging scores 1 of 4. Connect acting with who the reader is or aspires to be.

    Move: Frame action as identity

    For example

    Sign up for our newsletter.

    Rewrite

    Join 4,000 engineering leaders who get one practical idea every Tuesday.

  4. 6 · Pathos · Surprise, humour and delight · medium impact

    Surprise, humour and delight scores 1 of 4. Include one thing the reader would not predict: a counter-intuitive fact, a reversal, a reframe. Then pay it off.

    Move: Add a turn

    For example

    Most teams want to ship faster.

    Rewrite

    The fastest teams we studied ship less code, not more. Here's why.

  5. 7 · Logos · Objection handling · medium impact

    Objection handling scores 0 of 4. Name the sceptic's strongest objection and answer it with evidence. Two-sided beats one-sided for sceptical readers.

    Move: Raise and answer the objection

    For example

    (Price not mentioned)

    Rewrite

    Yes, we cost more than a freelancer. You are paying for a team that does not disappear in August.

  6. 8 · Logos · Contrast and alternatives · medium impact

    Contrast and alternatives scores 1 of 4. Compare with what the reader would do without you, including doing nothing, not just competitors.

    Move: Contrast with the status quo

    For example

    Our tool tracks expenses.

    Rewrite

    Instead of a shared spreadsheet that nobody updates, expenses are logged the moment a card is used.

  7. 10 · Kairos · The call to act · medium impact

    The call to act scores 0 of 4. End with a single, specific, low-friction action and say what happens when the reader takes it.

    Move: One clear next step

    For example

    Let us know if you have any questions.

    Rewrite

    Reply 'yes' and I will send two times for a 20-minute call next week.

  8. 11 · Kairos · Hope and agency · medium impact

    Hope and agency scores 1 of 4. After stating the stakes, show it can be done: a proof point, a first step, a reason for optimism. Urgency plus optimism moves people; urgency alone overwhelms them.

    Move: Pair urgency with hope

    For example

    Cyber attacks on firms like yours rose sharply last year. You are exposed.

    Rewrite

    Attacks on firms like yours are rising. The good news: three changes block most of them, and you can make the first one this week.

  9. 13 · Lenses and duties · Mythos · medium impact

    Mythos scores 0 of 4. Frame the message inside a bigger narrative: an old world, a shift, winners and losers, a promised land.

    Move: Tell the bigger story

    For example

    We sell sales software.

    Rewrite

    Buyers now do 70% of their research before talking to sales. The teams winning have stopped pitching and started teaching.

  10. 14 · Lenses and duties · Telos · medium impact

    Telos scores 1 of 4. Write the single sentence you want the reader to remember. Put it near the start, repeat it at the end, and cut anything that doesn't serve it.

    Move: Find the one big idea

    For example

    (Five equal points about features, pricing, history, team and roadmap)

    Rewrite

    One idea: we halve your onboarding time. Everything else is evidence for that.

  11. 2 · Ethos · Integrity and candour · low impact

    Integrity and candour scores 2 of 4. Delete self-awarded praise (world-class, revolutionary) and replace it with the fact that would have earned it.

    Move: Swap superlatives for specifics

    For example

    Our world-class support team is second to none.

    Rewrite

    A named engineer answers within 2 hours, 24/7.

  12. 3 · Ethos · Borrowed credibility · low impact

    Borrowed credibility scores 2 of 4. Replace vague praise with a named source, their role and the outcome they got.

    Move: Make proof specific

    For example

    "Great service, highly recommend!"

    Rewrite

    "We cut month-end close from 9 days to 3." Priya Shah, Finance Director, Northwind Logistics

  13. 9 · Logos · Mechanism and reasoning · low impact

    Mechanism and reasoning scores 2 of 4. After each claim, add 'because...' or 'which means...'.

    Move: Add the reason why

    For example

    Our coffee tastes better.

    Rewrite

    Our coffee tastes better because we roast to order and ship within 48 hours.

  14. 12 · Kairos · Fit to the moment · low impact

    Fit to the moment scores 2 of 4. Connect the message to a current shift, event or season the reader recognises.

    Move: Anchor in this moment

    For example

    AI can help your team.

    Rewrite

    Since your competitors started shipping AI features this year, buyers now expect them in every demo.

  15. 15 · Craft · Cohesion · low impact

    Cohesion scores 2 of 4. Link ideas explicitly with connectives, repeat key terms instead of synonyms, and start paragraphs by picking up the last thread.

    For example

    Costs rose. Our team is experienced. Clients like us.

    Rewrite

    Costs rose, so clients need a partner who can deliver for less. That's where our experience helps.

  16. 16 · Craft · Tone and voice · low impact

    Tone and voice scores 2 of 4. Pick one register for this reader and hold it; remove exclamation marks and hedges that undercut it.

    For example

    Hey!! Super excited to share our awesome proposal... We respectfully submit the following.

    Rewrite

    Thank you for the chance to propose. Here is what we suggest and why.

§ 10

Method and limitations

A quick scan: the model’s rules applied to the copy, with no AI reviewers.

Model: HowDoesThisLand persuasion model 0.5.1.

Scores are structured judgement, not predictions of persuasive effect. Meta-analyses show most message design choices have small, inconsistent effects (O'Keefe & Hoeken, 2021). Use the app to find weaknesses and options, then test with real readers.

Limitations

Message tweaks have small effects
Across 1,149 studies, the choice between message forms (story vs data, gain vs loss, one- vs two-sided) makes only a small and inconsistent difference. Who the audience is, what you offer and whether they trust you usually matter more than wording.
Some storytelling folk wisdom doesn't replicate
Popular claims such as 'loss framing beats gain framing', 'one story beats a statistic', 'emotion is 55% body language' and big nudge effects are weaker than often taught.
Kairos is now a fourth appeal
Aristotle's Rhetoric centres on three proofs; kairos comes from the sophists and wider Greek rhetoric. Treating it as a fourth appeal follows rhetorical scholarship that restores kairos to the centre, and catches the commonest failure in business writing: no reason to act now.
It judges the text, not the speaker
Aristotle's ethos is built in the speech, but real credibility also comes from prior reputation, brand and relationship. A brilliant cold email from an unknown sender still has an ethos ceiling. The relationship modifier partly compensates.
No explicit purpose or ask
A text can be credible, moving and logical and still persuade no one of anything, because it never asks.
Story is treated as a local technique
Pathos covers anecdotes, but persuasion often runs on a bigger worldview the audience joins (brands, movements, category design).
Style fit is missing
Aristotle himself added appropriateness (prepon) to style. Without it, the model may reward techniques that are wrong for the genre.
The categories overlap
A testimonial is ethos (borrowed credibility), logos (evidence) and pathos (story) at once. Forcing single labels loses information.
More is not always better
Maximising every appeal produces hype. The right mix depends on genre and reader; a CV with high pathos is a worse CV.
Persuasive is not the same as good
The appeals themselves are morally neutral. Optimised on their own, they can reward cleverness over honesty.
Culturally Western and verbal
The model reflects Greek rhetoric and mostly Anglo-American sales and marketing thinking. Directness, emotional display and authority work differently across cultures, and visual or spoken delivery is not assessed.
Uneven evidence base
Some underlying ideas are robust (anchoring, loss aversion), some mixed (scarcity, decoy, choice overload), some practitioner lore. Scores imply more precision than the science supports.
Culture changes the rules
Directness, use of evidence, hierarchy and emotional display vary by culture (Erin Meyer's Culture Map). A persuasive British landing page may read as pushy in Japan or vague in Germany.
Archetypes are simplifications
Real readers mix styles, and the same person reads differently under time pressure or in a group. The archetypes are built from research dimensions (need for cognition, regulatory focus, resistance to persuasion), but the five types themselves are a working model, not a validated instrument.
Culture is not destiny
Country profiles describe averages across millions of people, rest partly on decades-old survey data, and hide regional, generational and professional cultures. Adapting to cultural values gives a small, unreliable lift in experiments, and none at all in European and Latin American samples.
